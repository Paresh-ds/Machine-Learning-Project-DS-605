1. PROJECT PURPOSE
------------------
Dataset: UCI Appliances Energy Prediction (real-world building sensors and weather measurements, January-May 2016).
Goal: Understand household energy usage through data quality checking, exploratory data analysis, statistics, data mining, feature engineering, PCA, and machine learning.
Prediction target: Total_Energy = Appliances + lights.
Unit: Wh per 10-minute observation; after aggregating, Wh per 1-hour observation.
Important: The target measures recorded appliances + lighting consumption, not all possible home heating/cooling loads.

2. DATASETS / PROJECT FILES
---------------------------
df_10min: 19,735 observations. Most recent reported dataframe has 63 columns, including generated EDA flags and date/time columns.
df_1hour: Derived from df_10min for planned data mining, PCA and modelling.
Planned saved CSV: data/energy_1hour.csv
Notebooks: notebook/dataset_understanding.ipynb, notebook/EDA.ipynb
Plots: EDA_graph/ and/or notebook/EDA_graph/ (choose one consistent directory).

3. EDA WORKFLOW AND METHODS
---------------------------
- Inspected schema, data types, date/time ordering, missing data and duplicated records.
- Computed five-number summaries, means, medians, variances, standard deviations and skewness.
- Explored histograms, boxplots, daily patterns and differences across hours and weekends.
- Investigated IQR outliers: lower = Q1 - 1.5*IQR; upper = Q3 + 1.5*IQR.
- Compared high-energy events with sensor and weather flags using crosstabs and conditional rates.
- Calculated Pearson (linear), Spearman (monotonic) and Kendall (rank concordance) correlations.
- Investigated temporal dependence and autocorrelation.
- Statistical outlier does NOT automatically mean a sensor error. Leave original values unchanged unless evidence justifies a correction.

4. ENERGY-RELATED OBSERVATIONS
------------------------------
- At 10-minute resolution, Total_Energy = Appliances + lights.
- Historic 10-minute high-energy threshold used in EDA: Total_Energy > 175 Wh.
- High-energy spikes were explored by hour/day and temporal neighbors. Some jumps may be real events; some may warrant further validation.
- Hourly aggregation smooths short spikes but does not prove or correct measurement errors.
- Energy-derived outlier flags MUST NOT be used as model input predictors because of target leakage.
- Weekday/weekend high-energy-event percentages previously observed around 10.82% / 10.87%; that difference is small.

7. IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION RULES
--------------------------------
- Correlation does not mean causation.
- Overall near-zero correlation can coexist with patterns during particular conditions.
- Compare conditional percentages using each group's denominator, not raw overlap counts alone.
- Temperature and relative humidity are related physically; relative humidity is not the same as absolute moisture content.
- The 10-minute rows have time dependence; contiguous outliers are often a single event, not independent events.
- High energy consumption may reflect daily routines and weather confounding. Control for hour/season before drawing conclusions.
- Do not drop environmental outliers solely because they are beyond IQR fences.

8. HOURLY AGGREGATION PLAN (IMPORTANT)
--------------------------------------
Output dataframe: df_1hour.
Energy (Appliances, lights, Total_Energy): SUM of six 10-minute Wh observations.
Indoor T/RH and weather measurements: MEAN across six observations.
Feature-outlier flags: MAX across six observations (1 = at least one observation flagged within the hour).
Time-derived variables: rebuild from hourly datetime (hour, weekday, weekend).
Keep ONLY complete 1-hour bins of six 10-minute samples as previously planned.
Keep original 10-minute dataset and timestamps for traceability.
Flags generated at 10-minute scale describe any event WITHIN an hour; they are not the same as flags computed from the hourly-variable distribution.
Expected complete-hour count previously observed: ~3,289; verify actual after running aggregation.
Export from notebook working directory: ../data/energy_1hour.csv (verify Path.cwd()).

9. MODELLING / PCA PRECAUTIONS
------------------------------
- Split chronologically, not by random shuffling, for time-series energy prediction.
- Fit scaling, imputers, PCA, feature selection and any training-derived IQR thresholds on training data only; then transform validation/test.
- Avoid using Total_Energy, Appliances, lights, or flags calculated from these as input X when predicting Total_Energy.
- Avoid creating labels or features from the same hour's target when actual prediction is intended before observing that hour.
- Duplicate rv1 and rv2 convey no independent information; likely remove both as an evaluated experiment.
- A small individual correlation does not establish that a feature has zero incremental predictive value; compare validated models with and without features.
- Decide forecasting setup explicitly: predict energy in current hour from contemporaneously available inputs vs forecast NEXT hour from past information. This changes which predictors are legitimately available.
