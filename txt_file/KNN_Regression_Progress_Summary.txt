======================================================================
1. PROJECT OBJECTIVE
======================================================================
Predict hourly household Total_Energy (Wh) and investigate model
complexity, generalization, and errors using K-Nearest Neighbors
(KNN) Regression. Compare two PCA-based representations on the
same chronological observations.

Model A: 11 principal components from 24 room and weather features.
Model B: 6 principal components from 6 floor/weather features plus
         9 calendar features (15 model inputs in total).

KNN prediction: y_hat(x) = (1/K) * sum of target values for the K
nearest training observations. Euclidean distance is used after
appropriate feature scaling.

======================================================================
8. CONCLUSIONS AND LIMITATIONS
======================================================================
- KNN is highly flexible at small K and clearly overfit at K=1.
- Moderately large K gave more useful generalization in both models.
- Calendar-enriched Model B helped average cross-validation scores,
  but Model A was better on the held-out future time period.
- Both models were weak at predicting high-consumption episodes.
- KNN averages neighbor targets and can smooth out rare peaks.
- CV performance varied across folds; environmental conditions and
  household behavior may vary over time, but causes were not proven.
- Final R^2 values were low: do not claim strong predictive success.
- PCA files need not be changed; instead, refit scaler/PCA within
  each CV training fold using original feature-engineered inputs.
- We are predicting hourly consumption using same-time features;
  this is not yet a demonstrated future-ahead forecasting system.
- Avoid additional K tuning based on the held-out test set.
